## 1. Quick Take

The problem is real and the place makes sense. Hosur and Coimbatore have dense clusters of auto-component MSMEs under pressure from tier-1 suppliers and OEMs. But **MSME owners are among the hardest buyers in India**. They pay for things that cut the power bill or win an order, not for "OEE dashboards". This is also a hardware-and-field-service business wearing a SaaS costume.

## 2. The Five Fatal Questions

**Q1: Who is the customer, and would they pay for this today?**
- **The buyer:** owner of a **20–100 employee CNC or press shop in Hosur, Coimbatore or Peenya**, supplying TVS, Ashok Leyland, Titan, Bosch or a tier-1 supplier, with 10–40 machines.
- **Willingness to pay:**
  - **About ₹1,000–2,500 per machine per month**, but only if you can show power savings or downtime reduction within 60 days. The owner will haggle and want hardware free.
  - **"Clear OEM vendor audits"** is only worth paying for if the OEM *actually requires* it. Most don't yet.
- **Net:** this is a **maybe**, and it becomes a yes only with an ROI guarantee.

**Q2: Why hasn't someone built this already?**

Several have:
- **Infinite Uptime** (Pune): predictive maintenance using vibration sensors. It succeeded with **large plants** (steel, cement), not MSMEs.
- **Altizon, Entrib (ShopWorx) and Faclon Labs:** Indian Industrial IoT and MES platforms. They mostly sell to mid-to-large manufacturers, because MSME deals are too small to pay for field sales.
- **Zenatix:** IoT energy monitoring for many small sites. Schneider Electric took a majority stake in it, which shows *energy monitoring* is the part that sells.
- **Government programmes:** the SAMARTH Udyog / Industry 4.0 centres offer subsidised demos, which sets an expectation that this should be free.
- **What's different now:** sensors and edge hardware are cheap (₹3–5k per machine), and OEMs are starting to ask suppliers for carbon and quality data (supply-chain ESG disclosure). The timing is improving but hasn't arrived.

**Q3: What's the distribution advantage?**

There are partial ones:
- **Cluster density:** a single industrial estate holds 200+ shops.
- **Industry associations:** groups like CODISSIA and HOSIA help.
- **Word of mouth:** owners copy what their neighbours do.

But every install needs a site visit, an electrician and a follow-up. **The real growth loop is the OEM.** If TVS or Ashok Leyland asks its 300 suppliers to connect, the OEM does your selling.

**Q4: Can this be a big business, or is it a feature?**
- **It's a standalone business, but services-heavy.** Gross margins will look like hardware plus services (40–55%), not SaaS (80%).
- **$1M ARR (about ₹8.4 Cr)** at ₹2,500 per machine per month means **about 2,800 connected machines**, or about 200 factories at 14 machines each. That's achievable in one cluster over 3 years, *if* churn stays low.
- **The ceiling** is a solid ₹50–100 Cr company. It's not a unicorn unless it expands into financing or marketplace layers (for example selling spare machine capacity).

**Q5: Can the founder actually build this?**
- **Skills required:** electrical and OT engineering (PLC protocols such as Modbus, Fanuc FOCAS and Siemens S7), rugged hardware supply, installation crews and a dashboard.
- **Hardest challenge:** dealing with dozens of legacy machine variants, and getting owners to *act* on alerts.
- **The first hire is the problem:** an automation engineer from the local cluster who speaks Tamil or Kannada and is trusted on the shop floor. The cloud side is the easy part for this team.

## 4. Validation Experiments

**Experiment 1: Will owners pay for an energy audit?**
- **Test:** Will MSME owners pay for energy and uptime visibility?
- **How:**
  1. Buy 10 off-the-shelf Modbus energy meters plus a Wi-Fi gateway (about ₹12k total).
  2. Install them free for 7 days in 5 shops through a HOSIA or CODISSIA contact.
  3. Show each owner one sheet covering idle power, peak-demand penalties and machine idle hours.
  4. Ask for ₹2,000 per machine per month to continue.
- **Success:** At least 2 of 5 convert to paid, and at least one finding shows savings of ₹10k/month or more.
- **Time and cost:** 2 weeks, about ₹15,000 ($180).

**Experiment 2: Will an OEM fund supplier digitisation?**
- **Test:** Will a tier-1 supplier or OEM sponsor monitoring across its supplier base?
- **How:**
  1. Find supplier-development or purchase heads at 10 tier-1 or OEM firms around Hosur (TVS group companies, Ashok Leyland, Titan, Bosch, Caterpillar Hosur).
  2. Pitch "live delivery-risk and quality visibility across your 50 smallest suppliers".
- **Success:** At least 2 meetings and 1 request for a pilot proposal.
- **Time and cost:** 2 weeks, ₹2,000.

**Experiment 3: How hard is it to connect the machines?**
- **Test:** What share of real shop machines can you connect without opening the controller?
- **How:**
  1. Survey 50 machines across 5 shops: make, controller, age, and available ports (Ethernet, RS-232).
- **Success:** At least 60% are reachable with clamp-on sensors or standard protocols. If not, scope the product down to energy-only.
- **Time and cost:** 1 week, ₹3,000.

## 5. Verdict: Pivot It

**Pivot 1: lead with savings.** Sell *"we cut your power bill by 8–15% or you don't pay"*: energy and idle-time monitoring with a guaranteed outcome. Add predictive maintenance later.

**Pivot 2: sell to the OEM.** Make the **OEM or tier-1 supplier the paying customer** for supplier visibility. That turns 300 hard MSME sales into 1 enterprise sale with built-in rollout.
