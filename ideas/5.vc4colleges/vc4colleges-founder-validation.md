> **Founder profile used for this assessment:** Infra/Cloud Architects (datacenter, cloud, software, DevOps). Budget about ₹30L, to be spent in stages. Limited sales experience, willing to learn. Founders based in Bangalore, Australia and the US.

---

## 1. Bottom Line

Both versions of this idea struggle against the founder criteria. The **SaaS version** sells to colleges with tiny budgets and mandatory free government portals. The **fund version** spends the ₹30L directly on student startups with a 7–10 year wait for returns, and leaves nothing for the founders' own company. Neither uses the team's infrastructure expertise.

What the team *does* have is experience and a three-country network. That's valuable to students as **mentoring**, which costs time, not money.

## 2. Founder-Criteria Scorecard

| Criterion | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Domain Fit** | 1 | Academia, incubation management and early-stage investing are outside cloud and infrastructure. Building the software is easy, but knowing the buyer is not. |
| **Implementability** | 3 | The software could start as a simple hackathon-to-incubation tracker for one college. The fund could start with 2–3 small cheques. Both are easy to begin; neither shows progress quickly. |
| **Budget Scalability** | 2 | **SaaS:** cheap to build, but revenue per college is ₹25k–1L a year, so it never pays for itself. **Fund:** the budget *is* the product. ₹30L funds about 4–6 startups and then it's gone. |
| **AI-Native Potential** | 3 | AI mentor matching, idea scoring, pitch feedback and milestone tracking are good demos. No buyer has asked for them. |
| **Target Market** | 2 | Defined (IIC coordinators at Tier-2/3 colleges), but they're part-time faculty with annual budget cycles and need Principal or trustee approval. Many institutions, little money. |
| **Competitive Landscape** | 2 | Unstop, Devfolio and HackerEarth are free for students. The IIC portal, AICTE, Atal Innovation Mission and state missions (StartupTN, KSUM) cover reporting and grants for free. |
| **Remote Collaboration** | 3 | Software and mentoring can be remote, and founders in Australia and the US widen the mentor network. College relationships and events need someone in India. |
| **Total** | **16/35** | **Weak fit as a business** |

## 3. Budget View

| Version | Where the ₹30L goes | Expected return |
|---|---|---|
| **SaaS** | ₹5–10L to build and sell over 18 months | Probably under ₹10L a year in revenue, even with dozens of colleges |
| **Fund** | ₹30L across 4–6 startups at ₹5–7L each | Unknown, 7–10 years out, most cheques lost. Pooled money from several people may also need SEBI AIF compliance. |

Neither version keeps money in reserve for a company the founders could grow.

## 4. Closing the Sales Gap

Selling to colleges means slow, relationship-driven deals, academic-year timing and sometimes government tenders. That's a hard first sales job, and it doesn't teach skills the founders can reuse in B2B tech sales.

## 5. Working Across Bangalore, Australia and the US

The three locations are an asset here, but for mentoring, not for a business:
- Founders in Australia and the US can give students access to global markets, remote internships and international mentors.
- Video mentoring sessions fit easily across time zones.

## 6. A Better Way to Use This Idea

Keep the intent (helping Tier-2/3 talent) and link it to the main company:
1. **Talent pipeline:** run a small internship or "build with us" programme in 2–3 colleges. Students help build the main product (for example, compliance mappings or dashboards) and the best ones become your first hires.
2. **Give back without spending the budget:** founders mentor 1–2 student teams each, for a few hours a month.
3. **Revisit as an angel fund later**, once the main company earns money, as personal angel cheques rather than company funds.

## 7. Verdict

**Don't build this as the company.** Don't spend the ₹30L on it. Use the colleges as a hiring and mentoring channel for whichever idea the team picks.
